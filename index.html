Engineering craft: .NET Core and Clean Architecture

I build on .NET Core and organise code with Clean Architecture, so that the important business rules stay independent of frameworks, databases and screens.

  • Layers with one ruleDomain, application, infrastructure and presentation layers, with dependencies pointing inward only.
  • Business rules firstDomain-driven thinking: model the real business before choosing tools.
  • Tests that run fastBecause the core does not depend on a database, its automated tests are quick and run often.
  • APIsWeb APIs on ASP.NET Core that keep the outside world separate from the internal model.

New to the idea? Read Clean Architecture in .NET Core, explained simply.

Trading platform

SBAT: an AI-integrated automated trading platform I am building

SBAT is a personal engineering project. It is designed so that nothing acts without passing through checks, and so that the system can decide to do nothing.

  • Risk checks run before any order.
  • An abstention gate can decide not to trade, with reason codes.
  • A tested order state machine.
  • A market-calendar-aware data collector.

Built so far: the collector, the decision gate, the risk engine, the order state machine and an operator console, backed by hundreds of automated tests. Some console screens and the services behind them are deliberately marked planned until the backend exists, rather than showing screens with nothing behind them. It runs as paper trading only: no order is sent anywhere.

A personal research and engineering project. Not investment advice and not offered to others.

New to the subject? Read the plain-language series Trading explained: markets, orders, options, risk and how automated systems are built. General education only.

Forward Deployed Engineering: Built for the front line

I am interested in Forward Deployed Engineering: working close to the people who use the software, learning their domain and shipping something that works for them.

  • Understand the problem on siteI start from the real problem and the people who have it. My background in requirements analysis and delivery management helps me ask good questions and keep track of what has been decided.
  • Learn the domain fastI pick up new technologies and domains quickly. Over my career that has meant ASP.NET, Delphi, the MEAN stack, Xamarin and Azure, and today it includes industrial automation with PLCs and C#, including digital twins built with simulators.
  • Build and ship the working thingI take work from idea to release. On my own time I have designed and prepared 24 small Android apps end to end with AI-assisted development, including signing, store and release processes, and I am building a trading-platform engineering project.
  • Stay and iterate with the userI track status honestly and keep the loop short: put something in front of the user, learn from it and feed that back into the product.
