Radio Orbit Privacy Policy

Radio Orbit Privacy Policy

Effective date: 2026-11-27 App: com.sonith.radioorbit, version 0.5.0 (working name) Developer: Sonith Contact: sonythm@gmail.com

Short version

Your favourites, recent stations and settings stay on your phone. If you choose to use your location, it is used only on your phone and is not stored. If you choose a home area, only a rounded position (about 50 km) and its place name are saved on your phone; nothing is ever sent. The developer does not receive any of it and runs no server for this app. There are no accounts, no ads, no analytics and no crash reporting. To work, the app talks to two kinds of servers run by others: the Radio Browser station directory, and the stream server of the station you choose to play.

What the app stores on your device

  • A saved copy of the station list (name, place, stream address, logo address, genre tags, codec, bitrate, vote count), refreshed about once a day.
  • Your favourite stations and your recently played stations (up to 30).
  • Settings: whether the globe turns by itself, whether you chose to use your location (on or off only), your preferred languages (up to five), whether the first-run tips were shown, whether the controls start hidden, whether the Globe controls fade out after a few seconds, whether Calm mode is on, which tab the app opens on (automatic, Home or Globe), whether the one-time "What's new" tip was shown, whether Satellite view is on or off, whether the Station of the day card is shown, the Station of the day history (the last 14 days of daily picks: a date and a station identifier, kept only on the phone so the same station is not picked again soon), and your optional home area (a rounded position and a place name, see the Location section).
  • Your location itself (the coordinates your phone reports) is NOT stored. The only position kept is the coarse home area you choose yourself: see the Location section.

This is kept in a local database on the phone (SQLite). The app adds no encryption beyond your phone's storage protection.

Android Auto and the status-bar chip

  • Android Auto: if you use Radio Orbit in Android Auto, it shows your favourites, recently played stations and station lists on the car screen. These lists are read on your phone from the app's own local data; nothing is sent to us or anyone else. Playback uses the same station streams as the app and starts only when you choose a station or ask the Assistant.
  • Status-bar chip (Android 16 and later): the app can show a small ongoing notification with the station name and place while a station you started is playing. It is shown only on your phone, can be turned off in Settings or in Android settings, and sends nothing anywhere.

Station of the day and sharing

  • Station of the day is chosen on your phone from the saved station list, using the date, your preferred languages and your home area (if you set them). It makes no network request, sends no notification and runs nothing in the background. Its history is stored on the phone only and is removed by "Delete my data".
  • Share a station uses your phone's own share sheet. The app only hands the share sheet a short text (the station name, its place name as listed in the directory, and the station's own website address if the directory has one). It never includes your location, home area or coordinates. Nothing is sent until you choose an app and send it, and nothing is sent to the developer.

Network calls

  1. Radio Browser directory (radio-browser.info). The app asks all.api.radio-browser.info for the list of directory servers, then asks one of them for the list of stations and for your searches. When you press play, it also sends the directory's "station click" call with the station's identifier (the directory asks apps to do this to count listens). These servers receive your IP address and a User-Agent text "RadioOrbit/0.3", like any web request. Search words you type are sent to the directory as part of the search request. Their own policy applies: https://www.radio-browser.info
  2. The station you play. Audio streams come directly from each station's own server (often a streaming provider). That server sees your IP address, your device's request and when you listen, as with any website or radio app. The app does not choose or vet these servers. Their own policies apply.
  3. Song titles. While a station is playing and the app is open on screen, the app may open a second, short connection to the same stream server you are already listening to, asking it for the current song title (the standard Icy-MetaData header), about every 30 seconds. This goes only to that station's server (it sees your IP address, as it does for the audio) and the title is shown on the screen and the lock screen. Song titles are never sent anywhere else, are not looked up online and are kept only for the current session (the last 20). A "Search this song" button opens your browser only if you tap it.
  4. Station logos. The app may load a station's small logo image from the address given in the directory, which is a request to that logo's host.

The developer receives none of these requests.

Location

  • Optional. The app can centre the globe on where you are and list nearby stations. It asks first on its own screen ("Use my location" or "Not now") and only then shows the Android permission dialog. You can say no, and the app works fully; it then centres on your country, guessed from your phone's time zone and language, without using any location sensor.
  • It uses approximate (coarse) location only, while the app is open (permission ACCESS_COARSE_LOCATION). It never uses precise or background location.
  • Your position is processed on your phone only, to centre the globe and find nearby stations from the saved list. It is kept in memory for the current session only. It is not stored in the app's database, not written to logs, not sent to the developer, not sent to the Radio Browser directory and not sent to any station. Directory requests do not include it.
  • Optional home area: you can set a place as your home (from a place list, from a search result, or from the "Near you" list after using your location). The app then stores on your phone only the position rounded to 0.5 degrees (about 50 km, never exact coordinates) and the place name, for example "Mumbai, India", so the globe can open there and show a small house marker. It is never sent to the developer, to the Radio Browser directory or to any station, and no request includes it. In Settings, "Change home" lets you pick another place and "Forget home" deletes it. "Delete my data" removes it too.
  • Apart from the home area, the app remembers only your choice (on or off). In Settings you can switch "Use my location to centre the globe" off or use "Forget my location choice", and you can withdraw the permission in Android settings at any time.

Permissions the app requests

  • Internet: to load the station list, search, and play streams.
  • Foreground service (media playback) and wake lock: so audio continues with the screen off and the system can show the media notification.
  • Notifications (Android 13 and later): to show the playback notification and lock-screen controls. The app sends no other notifications. On Android 16 a further permission for ongoing "live" notifications (POST_PROMOTED_NOTIFICATIONS) lets the optional status-bar chip appear; it collects nothing.
  • Modify audio settings: used by the audio library for playback and audio focus.
  • Approximate location (ACCESS_COARSE_LOCATION): optional, see the Location section. Requested only when you tap "Use my location" or "Near me".
  • Network state and vibration: added by system libraries to detect connectivity and for button feedback; neither sends anything anywhere.

The app does not access precise or background location, contacts, microphone, camera, calendar, photos or files.

Sharing and sale of data

Nothing is sold or shared by the developer. There are no advertising or analytics SDKs.

Credits

Station data: Radio Browser (radio-browser.info), an open community directory. Land outlines: Natural Earth (public domain). Satellite view: imagery from NASA Visible Earth / Blue Marble (public domain), turned into coloured dots and bundled inside the app, so it is never downloaded and nothing is sent anywhere to show it. All are credited in the app under Settings, About.

Third-party content

Stations are operated by third parties. The app does not control, moderate or endorse their content, and it is not affiliated with any station or any other radio service. Content may not be suitable for everyone.

Backups

The app turns off Android Auto Backup for its data.

How to delete your data

  • In the app: Settings, "Delete my data" removes favourites, recents, settings and the saved station list. Remove a favourite with its heart. "Forget my location choice" resets the location choice. "Forget home" in Settings removes your home area. "Clear" in Recent removes the history.
  • Or uninstall the app, or clear its storage in Android settings.

The developer holds no copy, so there is nothing to request from the developer. Requests already sent to Radio Browser or to stations are governed by those operators.

Children

The app is aimed at ages 13 and over because stations are unmoderated third-party streams. It is not directed at children under 13. It collects no personal information and has no accounts.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be published at the same address with a new effective date.
