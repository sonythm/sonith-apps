What is Claude AI? A plain-language guide

Who makes Claude, what a language model is, what it can and cannot do, and how people use it, with the newest models as of 21 October 2026.

Reading level

If you have heard people say "ask Claude" and wondered what they mean, this guide is for you. No technical background is needed. By the end you will know what Claude is, who makes it, what it is good at, where it goes wrong, and how to try it safely. Each section gives you the plain-language picture first, then an optional Expert view for readers who want the precise details.

The short answer

Claude is an AI assistant. You type (or paste, or upload) something, and it writes back. You can ask it to explain an idea, draft an email, summarise a long document, look at a picture, or help write computer code. It is made by a company called Anthropic.

In plain words

Think of Claude as a very well-read assistant who works at the speed of typing. It has read an enormous amount of text and writes fluently about almost anything, and it can still make mistakes. You are the manager. It does the first draft, you check it.

Expert viewAnthropic and its stated mission

Claude is made by Anthropic, which describes itself as a public benefit corporation. Its company page states its mission as "AI research and products that put safety at the frontier". Its governance section mentions a Long-Term Benefit Trust and a board elected by stockholders. Those are the company's own statements about itself, so read them as intent, not as a guarantee of any particular outcome.

This guide is an independent article. I have no connection with Anthropic, and every fact below is linked to an official page at the end.

What is a language model?

Claude belongs to a kind of software called a large language model. The idea is easier than the name. Imagine your phone suggesting the next word as you type. Now imagine that suggestion engine trained on a huge library of books, articles, websites and computer code, and made far more capable. Given some text, it works out what text should come next, then the next piece, and so on. A reply is built one small piece at a time.

That simple loop turns out to be powerful. To continue text well, a model has to pick up grammar, facts, styles and patterns of reasoning. That is why one tool can help with a poem, a spreadsheet formula and a polite complaint letter.

Expert viewHow the "next piece" works

Models do not read letters or whole words. They read tokens, small pieces of text, and predict a probability for each possible next token, then pick one. Repeating that thousands of times produces a reply. Because the output is built from probabilities, the same question can produce differently worded answers, and a fluent answer is not the same thing as a verified one.

Modern assistants are trained further after the first stage so that they follow instructions and behave helpfully and safely. Anthropic describes its alignment, interpretability and Frontier Red Team work on its research pages. For you as a user, the practical lesson is the same: treat the output as a strong draft, not a final authority.

The Claude family, as of 21 October 2026

Anthropic does not make one Claude. It makes a family of models of different sizes, a bit like a range of cars. Its documentation lists four current models, and says all of them accept text and images and can use tools.

According to Anthropic's news page, Fable 5.1 was announced on 15 September 2026, Opus 5.5 on 22 September, Sonnet 5.5 on 28 September and Haiku 5.5 on 7 October. Anthropic also mentions Claude Mythos models with limited availability; I have not covered those. Lineups change often, so treat this as a snapshot and check the official page.

Expert viewReading the model table

Anthropic's overview lists a 1M-token context window for all four current models, a maximum output of 128K tokens per request, adaptive thinking, and a "reliable knowledge cutoff" of June 2026. The docs say 1M tokens is roughly 555,000 words on the current tokenizer. A knowledge cutoff is the date after which the model has no built-in knowledge, so anything newer must be supplied or searched.

Model IDs are pinned snapshots. The IDs follow the pattern claude-opus-5-5, claude-sonnet-5-5, claude-haiku-5-5 and claude-fable-5-1. Anthropic states retirement commitments (not sooner than September to October 2027 for these four on platforms it operates), which is useful when you build something that depends on a model. Prices are covered in part 4 of this series.

What Claude can do

Real example

A small business owner pastes in a long supplier contract and asks: "List the dates and amounts I must pay, in a table, and flag anything unusual." Claude produces the table in seconds. The owner still reads the flagged clauses personally before signing anything.

What Claude cannot do (or does badly)

  • It can be wrong. Models sometimes state invented facts, names or numbers with complete confidence. People call this "hallucination".
  • Its memory is limited. What it knows about your conversation is mostly what is on the page. Some products offer a memory feature.
  • It cannot see the future. Its reliable knowledge stops in June 2026 unless it is given newer information or can search.
Careful

For anything important, such as money, health, legal or safety decisions, treat Claude's answer as a draft to check against a trusted source.

Expert viewWhy errors are structural, not rare accidents

A model optimises for plausible continuations. When the training data is thin, or the question has no clear answer, a plausible-sounding but wrong continuation is still a high-probability one. That is why the failure is "confident": nothing in the output marks which sentences are shaky.

Anthropic says its latest models are less prone to hallucination than earlier ones and publishes prompting guidance such as asking the model to read the relevant files before answering about them. It also says, in the Opus 5.5 announcement, that the model often suspects it is being evaluated, which limits how well testing predicts real-world behaviour. Good practice follows from this: ask for quotes and sources, ask the model to say when it is unsure, and verify anything you will act on.

Claude Code and agents

Beyond chat, there is Claude Code, which Anthropic describes as an agentic coding tool that reads your codebase, edits files, runs commands and works with development tools. It runs in a terminal, in code editors, as a desktop app and in a browser.

An agent takes steps toward a goal instead of answering once: it looks, plans, acts and checks.

Anthropic's documentation says Claude Code can run several agents in parallel, with a lead agent coordinating. Later parts of this series cover context, CLAUDE.md, skills, sharing with a team, and agents in depth.

How Anthropic describes its approach to safety

Anthropic's research pages name teams working on alignment (helping models be helpful, honest and harmless), interpretability (studying how models work inside) and a Frontier Red Team that stress-tests systems. For Opus 5.5 the company says external evaluators tested the model before release, that it is more resistant to prompt injection than its predecessor, and that it is less likely to take hard-to-reverse actions.

How to start

  1. Open claude.ai and try a small, low-stakes task, such as summarising an article you already know.
  2. Be specific about what you want, and say who the answer is for.
  3. Check the result against what you know. Ask follow-up questions when something looks off.
  4. Move on to part 2 of this series for the basics that make answers better.
Glossary
Language model
Software trained on large amounts of text to continue text sensibly.
Token
A small piece of text, such as a word or part of a word, that the model reads and writes.
Prompt
What you type to Claude, including any files or instructions.
Hallucination
A confident-sounding answer that is false or invented.
Agent
An AI that takes several steps toward a goal, using tools, instead of answering once.
Context window
How much text the model can hold in view at one time.
Knowledge cutoff
The date after which the model has no built-in knowledge.

Frequently asked questions

Is Claude the same as ChatGPT?

No. They are different products from different companies, though both are language models and feel similar to use.

Is it free?

Anthropic offers a free plan and paid plans. Part 4 explains plans and pricing, with the official page linked.

Does Claude think?

It produces useful reasoning-like text, but whether that is thinking is a debate. In practice, treat it as a capable tool, not a person.

Can I trust it?

Trust it as you would a quick, clever colleague: useful, but verify what matters.

Key takeaways

  • Claude is an AI assistant made by Anthropic
  • It writes fluently but can be confidently wrong
  • Start with low-stakes tasks and check anything important
