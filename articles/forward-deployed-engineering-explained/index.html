Forward Deployed Engineering explained

What a forward deployed engineer does, where the term comes from, how the role differs from nearby roles, and how AI is making it more important.

  • It differs from a software engineer, consultant or product manager

Reading level

Most software is built far away from the people who use it. A forward deployed engineer (FDE) does the opposite: they go to where the problem is, work beside the people who have it, and build or adapt software until it works in the real world. The role has been getting a lot of attention, so this article explains it plainly, with a picture first and an Expert view in each section.

The short version

An FDE is a software engineer embedded with customers. They learn how a customer's business really runs, make technology work inside it, and stay close enough to see whether it helped.

Expert viewA working definition

There is no single official definition. A practical one: an engineer who is accountable for a customer's outcome with the product, not only for the product's code, and who therefore combines engineering depth with customer-facing work. Anthropic's announcement of its Claude Frontier Academy (below) describes FDEs as engineers who can "take Claude from an idea to a system in production", working inside client organisations from use-case selection through security review to handover.

The chef analogy

In plain words

The product team builds the kitchen and the recipes. The forward deployed engineer takes them into your kitchen and makes the meal work for your table.

Where the term comes from

The practice is most closely associated with Palantir, a US software company, which has used the title Forward Deployed Software Engineer for engineers who deploy its platforms at customers, and nicknames the role "Delta" inside the company. Embedding engineers with customers is older than any single company, for example vendors who sent engineers to customer sites to install and customise systems, but the FDE label spread widely after Palantir's use of it. This article has no connection with Palantir.

Expert viewOn certainty

I have not been able to confirm exactly when the public term first appeared, and the Palantir engineering blog page I tried to read returned an access error, so my description of its "Delta" naming rests on search summaries and should be treated with care. The reliable claims are the modest ones: the label is widely associated with Palantir, and embedding engineers at customer sites predates it.

What the work looks like

Real example

A warehouse team re-enters the same details in two systems every day. An FDE spends a day on the floor, sees where the double entry happens, builds a small connector, tries it with one shift, and adjusts it after the team points out an exception nobody had mentioned.

Expert viewWhy the loop is short on purpose

The shorter the distance between a real problem and a working solution, the faster everyone learns. Experts add two disciplines: write down what you learn so the product team can generalise it (otherwise every deployment is a one-off), and decide early what is a customer-specific adaptation versus a product gap. Without that line, FDE teams drift into becoming a bespoke consultancy.

How it differs from nearby roles

RoleWhat they mainly doTime with customers
Software engineerBuilds the product for many customers, usually from a distance.Little with any one customer
Forward deployed engineerBuilds and adapts software for specific customers, on site or close by.A lot
Solutions engineerOften supports sales: demos the product, designs how it would fit, helps with early set-up.A lot, mostly before and during set-up
ConsultantAdvises on strategy or process, and may not build the software itself.A lot
Product managerDecides what the product should do, across all customers.Some
Expert viewBoundaries are fuzzy

Titles vary between companies, and some people do several of these jobs. A useful test is what you are held accountable for: a software engineer for the quality of the product, a solutions engineer for the quality of the fit before the sale, a consultant for the quality of advice, a product manager for the quality of the product decisions, and an FDE for whether the customer actually succeeds with the software in production.

Skills that matter

Why AI makes the role more important

Anthropic's announcement of its Claude Frontier Academy on 2 October 2026 gives one view. It says the main obstacle to enterprise AI is talent: the people who can make AI work inside a real business, whom it calls the "hardest talent to find".

Expert viewThe academy's structure, as announced

Anthropic describes the first programme, the FDE Residency, as modelled on medical training with three stages: a multi-day in-person programme with a simulated enterprise deployment and a graded practical (earning a Claude Resident Engineer badge); a 12-week residency in which engineers lead a real Claude use case at their own organisation, supported by Anthropic engineers; and a final assessment (earning a Claude Frontier Deployed Engineer badge, the first expected in early 2027). Cohorts are running in San Francisco, New York and London, and participation is by nomination from organisations. Candidates are described as hands-on software engineers with strong fundamentals, a record of building with LLMs and experience helping others adopt AI. This is Anthropic's own programme and its own description; treat it as one company's view of the role.

Stay close to the problem, build something real, and bring what you learn back.

How to become one

Glossary
Forward deployed
Working at or close to the customer's site rather than in the company's office.
Embedded
Sitting within the customer's team.
Prototype
A quick early version built to test an idea.
Deployment
Putting software into real use.
Domain
The field a customer works in, such as logistics or banking.
Stakeholder
Anyone affected by or interested in the solution.

Frequently asked questions

Is forward deployed engineering a sales job?

No. It is an engineering job done close to customers. It can support sales, but the work is building and fixing.

Do FDEs travel a lot?

It depends on the company and the customers. Some work on site, some remotely with frequent contact.

Do I need a computer science degree?

Many FDEs have one, but what matters most is that you can build, learn fast and communicate. Check the requirements of any role you are interested in.

Is the role only for big technology companies?

No. Any organisation that rolls out complex software to customers may use some version of it.
